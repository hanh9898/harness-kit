## Original research inspected

**Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens (2000), A Model for Types and Levels of Human
Interaction with Automation**, IEEE 30(3), 286–297,
[DOI 10.1109/3468.844354](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354).
The [indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11760769/) corroborates the
four functions. Section III, Table I and Figure 2 distinguish types and levels;
[SCALE.md](SCALE.md) paraphrases the inspected original table. Publisher full text
was access-challenged and the old author-lab URL unavailable. A
[mirrored original PDF](https://www.geocities.ws/tgsrao/parasuraman.pdf), with matching
authors, publication details and printed pages, supplied the full text and visual
table inspection. Mirror integrity is not publisher-authenticated. The procedure's
per-task ceiling records are an adaptation, not fields prescribed by that paper.

**Shavit et al. (2023), Practices for Governing Agentic AI Systems**,
[official paper](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/practices-for-governing-agentic-ai-systems.pdf),
sections 4.2, 4.4 and 4.7. It discusses action constraints, legibility and action
ledgers, review, and interruption; it also cautions that retrospective explanations
can mislead. This kit uses attributable action/effect evidence with concise decision
rationale, not a demand for hidden reasoning or a claim that a rationale proves
causation. Its pre-action capture, prior-authority and logical-history requirements
come from the accepted spec. The paper does not require this kit's storage method,
a new approval system or a universal record schema.

**Lee and See (2004), Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance**,
Human Factors 46(1), 50–80,
[DOI 10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392).
The original article on [John Lee's author-associated Academia page](https://www.academia.edu/515392/Trust_In_Automation_Designing_for_Appropriate_Reliance)
was inspected, especially pp. 53–56 and 59–61. Publisher PDF timed out and the old
Iowa laboratory PDF returned 404. Trust is an attitude; reliance is behavior.
Calibration concerns fit to capability, with resolution and functional/temporal
specificity. Performance, process and purpose inform trust. The kit adapts these
ideas to evidence-scoped recommendations, not a claimed validated authority metric.
It supplies no universal numeric promotion threshold.

## Migration and qualification limits

The historical four-axis/seven-step material remains `partial`; its original
unread-source statements describe that earlier research. Fresh inspection above
neither rewrites history nor upgrades every historical claim. Reversibility,
information sufficiency and permission boundaries remain useful considerations;
old percentage/refusal-count heuristics and step conversions are not PSW or Lee–See
calibration rules. A selected consumer policy requires its own authorized migration.

Qualified alternatives preserve concrete authority meaning and applicable controls;
no alternative is prequalified by being named. Method/provider invocation,
instructed behavior, native enforcement and observed outcomes require their own
conditions and evidence. Local tests cannot establish another host's enforcement,
a shared deployment or general improvement.
