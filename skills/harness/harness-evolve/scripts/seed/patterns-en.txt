Single agent with tools || base level; direct model call without an agent is lower still || One agent that uses tools in a loop to finish a task. || The task fits one context and one role; always try this first. || The work has parts that can run in parallel or needs separate trust boundaries. || stated
Sequential chain (pipeline) || prompt chaining; sequential orchestration || Fixed steps where each step's output feeds the next. || Steps are known up front and each is easier once the previous one is done. || Steps depend on the input in ways you cannot predict, or the order must change at run time. || stated
Parallel fan-out and gather || parallelization; concurrent orchestration || Independent sub-tasks run at the same time and their results are merged. || Parts are independent, or several perspectives on the same input add confidence. || Parts edit the same files or depend on each other; the token cost is multiplied. || stated
One-shot router || routing; coordinator; deterministic dispatch || A first step classifies the input and sends it to one specialist path. || Inputs fall into distinct categories that need different handling. || Categories overlap or the best path is only known during the work; a plain rule-based dispatcher may do. || stated
Peer handoff || handoffs; dynamic delegation || Control moves between peer agents; one agent is active at a time. || The right specialist emerges during the conversation. || The route can be decided from the input at the start; then a router is simpler. || stated
Orchestrator with workers || manager; supervisor; coordinator; orchestrator-workers || One central agent keeps authority, splits the task, delegates to workers and combines results. || Sub-tasks are not known in advance and one party must own the result. || The task is small, or workers need to share context continuously. || stated
Multi-level hierarchy || hierarchical teams; hierarchical task decomposition || Orchestrators that themselves delegate to further orchestrators or workers. || The task is too large for a single orchestrator's context or tool set. || Two levels are enough; each extra tier adds cost and loses detail. || stated
Maker and checker || evaluator-optimizer; reviewer and critic; reflection || One agent produces and another grades against criteria, looping until it passes or a cap is reached. || Clear quality criteria exist and iteration measurably improves the result. || The criteria are unclear or the loop has no cap; escalate to a person when the cap is hit. || stated
Loop with a stop condition || iterative refinement; bounded iteration || Repeat a step until a condition holds or a maximum number of rounds is reached. || Work converges by repetition, such as fix-and-retest. || There is no checkable stop condition; always set a round cap. || inferred
Group discussion || group chat; all-to-all collaboration; debate || Several agents exchange views in a shared thread, often moderated. || Brainstorming, review from several viewpoints, or challenge between teammates. || You need a quick, deterministic answer; keep the group small (about three agents in one source). || stated
Fixed graph or coded workflow || DAG; custom logic; workflow as code || The control flow is written in code and agents are nodes in it. || The process is well understood and you want repeatable control over order. || The path must adapt freely to what the agent finds. || stated
Planner and task ledger || magentic orchestration; planning; task list || A planner builds and updates a ledger of tasks that workers claim and complete. || Open-ended work where the plan must be revisited as results arrive. || Short tasks where the planning overhead exceeds the work. || inferred
Human in the loop || approval gate; checkpoint; escalation target || Defined points where a person reviews, approves or gives feedback before work continues. || Subjective judgment, high stakes, or an action that is hard to undo. || The step is routine and reversible; a mandatory gate makes that step synchronous, so persist state there. || stated
ReAct loop || autonomous agent; free-running tool loop || The agent alternates reasoning and tool calls, deciding each next step itself until it judges the task done. || Open-ended problems where the steps cannot be written down in advance and the environment gives feedback each step. || Errors are costly or compound; add stopping conditions, a turn cap and checkpoints with a person. || stated
